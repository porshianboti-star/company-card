Why a designer's card has to carry the portfolio

An interior designer is hired on two things: what your work looks like, and who vouched for you. A paper card carries neither. It has your name and a number, and the person holding it still has to go and find your portfolio before they can decide whether to call.

A digital card puts the portfolio one tap away. A homeowner scans the QR code at the end of a consultation and lands on your card with your project photos, your Instagram or Pinterest and your website, then saves your number without typing it. A realtor or contractor who refers you can forward the link in a message instead of describing you from memory.

The details designers hand out also change: a new studio address, a new showroom, a new booking link, a portfolio that has three better projects than it had last spring. A printed card is out of date the day that happens. A live card is edited once, and every QR code and link you have already given out shows the current version.

What an interior designer's card should carry

  • What you design and for whom. “Residential interiors and kitchen renovations in the Bay Area” or “Commercial fit-outs for cafés and boutiques” tells a prospect in one line whether you are the right call.
  • Your studio name and logo, or your own name if you work alone. With your website, so a referral can check your work before they get in touch.
  • Your portfolio and social links. The project gallery on your site, your Instagram, Pinterest or Houzz, and anywhere else your work is photographed. On the free plan the card holds your profile, links and socials; Pro adds unlimited links and files, so a PDF lookbook can sit on the card too.
  • A consultation booking link. The scheduling link you already use for a first meeting or a site visit.
  • Any membership or certification you already show elsewhere. Whatever your professional association or jurisdiction expects — CompanyCard does not check or supply these, so add them exactly as they appear on your website.
  • A direct number and an email address. For the contractor on site who needs you now, not a form.

Setting up your card in ten minutes

  1. Open the builder and add your name, studio name, direct number and the kind of work you take on and where.
  2. Add your portfolio, Instagram, Pinterest or Houzz and your consultation booking link, plus any membership or certification you already show on your website.
  3. Print the QR code on your proposals, your showroom stand and the back of your existing cards.
  4. Paste the card link into your email signature, add the card to Apple or Google Wallet, and send the link to the realtors and contractors who refer you.

Frequently asked questions

What should an interior designer's digital business card include?

What you design and for whom, your studio name and logo or your own name, your website and portfolio, your Instagram, Pinterest or Houzz, a consultation booking link, any membership or certification you already show elsewhere, and a direct number and email.

Can I put my portfolio on the card?

Yes. On the free plan the card carries your profile, links and socials, so your portfolio page and your project galleries are one tap away. Pro adds unlimited links and files, so a PDF lookbook or a project sheet can sit on the card as well.

What happens to the cards I have already handed out when my studio moves?

Nothing needs reprinting. The QR code and link point to your live card, so you edit the address once and every card, brochure and email you have already sent shows the new details.

Do clients need an app to open my card?

No. The card opens in any browser from a QR code or a link, and saving you to their contacts is one tap. Only you need an account.

Is it free for a self-employed interior designer?

Yes. The free plan includes one digital business card, a QR code and sharing link, your profile, links and socials, an Apple and Google Wallet pass, and unlimited updates — free forever, no credit card. Free cards carry a small CompanyCard credit; removing it is part of Pro. Lead capture, analytics, custom branding and unlimited links and files are part of Pro.
