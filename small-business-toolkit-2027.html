The practical small business toolkit for 2027

A small business or freelancer planning their 2027 stack can cover the essentials — sharing contact details, scheduling meetings, invoicing, project tracking, passwords, and design — for $0 a month to start, using free tiers that the vendors themselves publish, and then pay only for the one or two tools that earn an upgrade. That is the whole strategy in one sentence. The rest of this article is the specific tools, their real limits, and what the first paid tier costs.

A note on method: every third-party price and limit below was checked against the vendor's live pricing page in August 2026. Prices change, so treat these as a verified starting point, not a permanent guarantee — and check the linked pages before you commit a 2027 budget line. One tool below is our own product, and we say so where it appears.

Your digital identity when meeting people

Paper cards go stale the day your phone number or role changes. A digital business card is a link and a QR code that always point to your current details, so it is a sensible default for anyone planning how they will introduce themselves in 2027.

Disclosure: CompanyCard is our product. CompanyCard's free plan is $0 forever and includes one digital business card with a QR code and sharing link, an Apple and Google Wallet pass, and unlimited edits — no credit card required, and the person receiving your card needs no app. The QR code and link are permanent, so a code printed on a flyer or van keeps working after you edit the card. The honest trade-off: the free plan is one card and carries a small CompanyCard credit. Paid plans are published at $7.99/month for Pro ($5.99/month billed annually), and team plans have no seat minimum.

The honest rival to name is Blinq. Its free plan is ahead of ours on card count — two free digital business cards to our one — and also includes an email signature, virtual backgrounds, and Apple and Google Wallet passes. Blinq Premium is $9.99/month billed monthly ($7.33/month billed annually); its Business plan is $6.99 per user/month ($4.99 billed annually), and Blinq's pricing page states that a minimum payment equal to 5 Team Cards is required for all Business subscriptions — which matters if your "team" is two people. Solo and want two free cards? Blinq is a fair pick. Small team that cannot fill a five-card minimum? That is where CompanyCard fits.

Scheduling, plus a simple CRM

The back-and-forth of booking meetings is the first thing worth automating. Calendly has a plan that is always free; the limit to know is that it allows one event type — a single kind of bookable meeting, such as "30-minute intro call." For most freelancers that is genuinely enough. When you need multiple event types, the Standard plan is $10 per seat per month billed monthly.

Once bookings turn into a pipeline, pair it with HubSpot's free CRM: free for up to 2 users with no credit card required, and you can add 1,000 contacts with no time limit. That is a real working CRM at $0, not a trial. HubSpot's Starter tier lists at $20/month per seat, though the page was showing a discounted promotional rate in August 2026 — budget against the list price.

Invoicing and accounting

Two credible free routes here, with different fine print.

Wave Accounting's Starter Plan is $0 and covers invoicing and basic accounting. The limit that bites: automatic bank-transaction imports are a Pro Plan feature, so on the free plan you enter or upload transactions yourself. Pro is $19 USD/month billed monthly.

Zoho Invoice is simply free — Zoho removed its price tag after 13 years as a paid tool. The free plan sends up to 500 invoices per year and allows up to two users, which comfortably covers most solo operations. Two things to know before relying on it: if you are inactive for more than 180 days, Zoho states your associated data will be deleted within the next 20 days — so an occasional-use business should export copies. And there is no paid upgrade path within Zoho Invoice itself; when you outgrow it, Zoho points you to a separate product (Zoho Billing) rather than a bigger tier of the same one.

Project boards

Trello's free tier covers up to 10 collaborators per Workspace and up to 10 boards per Workspace — enough for a small business to run "Jobs this week," "Marketing," and a client board or two without paying. If you outgrow ten boards, the Standard plan is $5 per user per month billed annually. Boards are the least glamorous tool on this list and often the one a two-person business actually opens every day.

Passwords

By 2027 your business will sit behind a few dozen logins, and reusing one password across them is a common way small businesses get breached. Bitwarden's basic password management is always free, and the free tier includes sharing with one other user — enough for a two-person business to share the bank login and the social accounts safely. Premium is $1.65 per month billed annually, which is likely the smallest line item in your entire 2027 budget.

Design

Canva Free is available to anyone and handles the flyers, social posts, and price lists a small business actually produces. The limit worth knowing going into 2027 is on the AI side: the free plan's AI allowance covers Standard and Premium AI only, without the Ultra AI features paid plans get. Canva Pro is US$120/year for one person on yearly billing. One caution from our own field: don't build your email signature as a Canva image — an image signature isn't clickable and disappears when a recipient blocks images.

FAQ

Can a small business really run on free tiers through 2027?

An early-stage one can, if the limits fit: one Calendly event type, 500 Zoho invoices a year, 10 Trello boards, one or two free business cards. The pattern across the vendors here is that the free tier covers a real solo workload, and each paid tier starts where one specific limit bites — more event types, automatic bank imports, more boards, more cards. Plan to pay for the one tool you hit the ceiling on, not all of them.

What is the catch with free plans?

Each has a specific, published one, and they are listed above rather than hidden: Wave's free plan lacks automatic bank imports, Zoho Invoice deletes data after 180 days of inactivity, Calendly free is one event type, and our own free card carries a small CompanyCard credit while Blinq's free plan offers two cards to our one. Read the limit before you build a workflow on it.

Why pick tools now for 2027?

Because switching costs grow with use: a QR code already printed on materials, a year of invoices in one system, a board layout your team knows. Choosing in Q4 — against verified current pricing — means you start January on tools you have already tested, rather than migrating in your busiest quarter. Verify the prices above on each vendor's page when you decide; this article's figures were checked in August 2026.
