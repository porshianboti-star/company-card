Why a property manager's contact details matter more than most

A property manager is the one contact three different groups need at short notice: the owners whose properties you look after, the tenants who live in them, and the contractors you send to fix things. Each of them needs a different link from you — a portal, a maintenance request form, an emergency number — and each of them loses the paper card.

A digital card puts all of those on one page. A tenant scans the QR code on the welcome pack or the noticeboard and saves you to their phone in one tap; an owner gets the same link in an email; a contractor has your direct number before they reach the door.

Managers' details change more than most: a new office number, a new portal or booking link, a new emergency line, a property added to the portfolio. A printed card in every welcome pack is wrong the day that happens. A live card is edited once, and every QR code and link you have already handed out shows the new details.

What a property manager's card should carry

  • Who you manage for and where. “Residential lettings and block management in the north of the city” tells an owner in a second whether you cover their property.
  • Your business name and logo, or your own name if you work independently. With your website, so an owner can check who you are before they call.
  • A tenant link and an emergency line. Your maintenance request form or tenant portal, and the number to call out of hours, labelled so nobody uses the wrong one.
  • An owner link. A booking link for a valuation or first meeting, or the owner portal you already use.
  • Any licence or registration details you are expected to show. Whatever your jurisdiction or professional body asks for — CompanyCard does not check or supply these, so add them exactly as you show them elsewhere.
  • A direct number and an email address. For the contractors and owners who need you rather than a form.

Setting up your card in ten minutes

  1. Open the builder and add your name, business name, direct number and the areas and property types you manage.
  2. Add your tenant link and emergency number, an owner booking link, and any licence or registration details you are expected to show — label each link for who it is for.
  3. Print the QR code in your welcome packs and on communal notices.
  4. Paste the card link into your email signature, add the card to Apple or Google Wallet, and send the link to your owners and regular contractors.

Frequently asked questions

What should a property manager's digital business card include?

Who you manage for and where, your business name and logo or your own name, a tenant link such as a maintenance request form or portal, an emergency number labelled as such, a booking link for owners, any licence or registration details you are expected to show, and a direct number and email.

Can tenants and owners see different links on one card?

They see the same card, so label each link for who it is for — for example “Tenants: report a repair” and “Owners: book a valuation”. On the free plan you add your profile, links and socials; Pro adds unlimited links and files.

What happens to the QR codes I have already handed out when my number changes?

Nothing needs reprinting. The QR code and link point to your live card, so you edit the card once and every welcome pack, notice and email you have already sent shows the new details.

Do tenants need an app to open my card?

No. The card opens in any browser from a QR code or a link, and saving you to their contacts is one tap. Only you need an account.

Is it free for an independent property manager?

Yes. The free plan includes one digital business card, a QR code and sharing link, your profile, links and socials, an Apple and Google Wallet pass, and unlimited updates — free forever, no credit card. Free cards carry a small CompanyCard credit; removing it is part of Pro. Lead capture, analytics, custom branding and unlimited links and files are part of Pro.
