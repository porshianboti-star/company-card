How a landscaper actually gets the next job

Most landscaping work comes from being seen doing it. Your crew spends a week on one front garden in full view of the street, and the neighbour two doors along wants the same. But the person she can reach is the crew lead unloading the trailer, because you are across town pricing a patio. So the card has to be on the truck door, the trailer gate and the yard sign left in the bed for the week after, where she can scan it without stopping the crew.

Whether they scanned the trailer or had your link forwarded by the friend who asked who laid the pavers, a homeowner settles three things before calling a landscaper: your area, your kind of job (a weekly mow is not a design-and-build garden), and whether you are insured and licensed for it. A paper card from the truck cannot keep those answers current; a digital card can, and because the QR code, link or email signature all open the live version, the neighbour who scanned in March sees what you are booking in July.

What goes on a landscaper's card

  • The company and the owner, both named. Neighbours remember the truck; property managers remember who walked the site.
  • Exactly which landscaping work you take on. Mowing and maintenance, clean-ups, planting, patios and walls, irrigation, tree work, snow clearance. A homeowner rules you in or out from this line.
  • The towns your crews cover and whether you are booking this season. Mowing routes fill by spring and install calendars by early summer; say what you are taking on in the tagline.
  • A quote or estimate request link. Link your estimate form or a site-visit booking page, so the neighbour asks for a price while your work is in front of them.
  • Photos of finished gardens. Link the portfolio page or Instagram account where your job photos live; homeowners hire the landscaper whose last garden looks like theirs.
  • Licence and insurance. General liability and workers' compensation, plus your pesticide applicator or contractor licence number if your state wants it on advertising. A homeowner about to let a skid steer onto the lawn wants to know.

What a property manager or trade partner needs to see on a landscaper's card

The steadier money in landscaping is a grounds contract for a homeowners association or an apartment complex, awarded by a property manager who has never seen your work and checks paperwork before portfolio: a certificate of insurance, workers' compensation for the crew, and whether you cover the whole year from spring mulch to winter salt.

So the card should say which commercial grounds work you take, that you are insured and can send a certificate on request, and how to book a site walk without ringing a phone the crew answers from a mower. The realtor tidying a listing before photographs, the builder finishing a lot and the nursery asked who can put the plants in want the same thing in a form they can forward, so email them the link and put it in your email signature.

What changes across a landscaping season and has to stay current

A landscaping business is a different business each quarter. Spring is clean-ups, mulch and the scramble to fill the mowing route. Summer is installs and irrigation. Autumn is leaf clearance and the last patios before the ground freezes. Winter is snow or nothing, and the number that matters is the plow crew's.

Between seasons a crew lead leaves with his number, you extend the area a town north, or you drop tree removal after the insurance quote. All of it is printed on truck magnets, the trailer wrap, yard signs and the estimate pad, and nobody reprints the trailer because the mowing route is full. With a digital card the QR code on each of those opens one live card: change the tagline to autumn clean-ups once and the yard sign in a client's bed since June is right again.

Licences, pesticide certification and what your state wants on landscaping ads

Landscaping is lightly regulated, but the regulated parts show up on marketing. Spraying herbicide or insecticide for hire usually needs a commercial pesticide applicator licence from your state's agriculture department, and some states expect that number on advertising for the service. Hardscape or irrigation above a certain value needs a contractor licence in some states and none in others, and some want the number shown.

The rules differ by state and county, so ask your state's licensing board or agriculture department rather than copying another landscaper's truck. If you are not licensed to spray, describe your lawn care without implying you do, and if the rules or your number change, correct the card once and every printed truck magnet and yard sign shows the new wording.

Setting up a landscaper's card

  1. Open the builder and enter your name, your role (owner, foreman, landscape designer) as the job title, and the company name as it reads on the truck.
  2. Add the office phone and the crew lead's number, the towns you cover, and link your estimate request form or site-visit booking page as the card's booking link.
  3. Put what you are booking this season and your services in the tagline, add your licence number and an insured line if your state wants them, and link your portfolio page or Instagram gallery.
  4. Add your logo and set the theme colour to match the truck, then print the QR code on truck magnets, the trailer and yard signs, email the link to property managers and trade partners, and add it to your email signature.

Frequently asked questions from landscapers

What should a landscaper's digital business card include?

The company and owner's name, the landscaping work you take on, the towns you cover, whether you are booking this season, a quote request link, a link to photos of finished gardens, and your insurance and licence details.

How does a landscaper use the QR code on the truck and at job sites?

Print it on the truck door magnets, the trailer gate and the yard signs you leave after a job. A neighbour who has watched the work go in scans it from the street and requests a quote while the crew is still there.

Can I change my services after the QR code is printed on yard signs and truck magnets?

Yes. The QR code and link open the landscaping card itself, not a copy of it, so mark the mowing route full, add snow clearance or change the crew lead's number and every yard sign, truck magnet and estimate pad already out there shows the new version.

Do I need to show a licence number on a landscaping business card?

It depends on the landscaping work and the state. Many states require a commercial pesticide applicator licence to spray lawns for hire and some want that number on advertising; a few require a contractor licence for hardscape or irrigation. Ask your state's licensing board or agriculture department, and if your number changes, edit the card once.
