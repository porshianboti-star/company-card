How a mobile notary actually gets signings

Almost nobody finds a notary the way they find a restaurant. An escrow officer has a closing at five and a borrower who can only sign at home in the next county. A signing service dispatcher is working down a list of agents who cover that ZIP code. A care home social worker needs a power of attorney witnessed this afternoon. Each time, the booker has a phone in hand and needs to know within a minute whether you are commissioned, insured and close enough.

That is the job the card has to do. It gets texted to an escrow officer by the agent who used you last month, emailed to a vendor coordinator adding you to a rotation, and scanned off the folder you left on a title company's front desk. Whoever opens it has to see your commission state and expiry, whether you take loan signings, and a number that connects without a search.

Repeat work follows the same path. The borrower you met at the signing table refinances in two years and asks for you by name; the paralegal who used you for an estate plan has another next month. Because the QR code, link and email signature all open the live card, the copy they saved still shows a current commission when they call.

What goes on a notary's card

  • Commission state, number and expiry date. The first thing a title company checks. Update it the day the renewal certificate arrives.
  • Loan signing agent status. Whether you handle loan packages, whether your background screening is current, and whether you can print on letter and legal paper and scan back the same night.
  • E&O insurance and the amount. Signing services and many title companies will not book an agent without it. A bond is a different thing; name both if you carry both.
  • Remote online notarization. If your state has authorised you for RON, say so and name the platform. If you are mobile only, say that too, so nobody books the wrong kind of appointment.
  • Service area and travel policy. The counties or radius you cover, whether you go into hospitals, care homes and jails, and how you quote travel. Check your state's rule on which fees a notary may charge.
  • Availability and a direct line. Evenings, weekends and same-day are where a mobile notary earns. A tap-to-call number beats a contact form.
  • Languages and specialties. Spanish-language signings, apostille handling, estate planning packages, vehicle title transfers, so the right jobs reach you.

What a title company or signing service checks before booking a signing agent

When a signing service or escrow office takes you on, the person doing it is usually a vendor coordinator with a checklist, not a client who will ever meet you. They check that the commission is valid in the property's state and not about to lapse, that your E&O meets their minimum, that a background screening is current, and that you can print a full loan package and return scan-backs before funding.

Put those answers where they open first. A card that reads commission expiry, E&O amount, background screened, dual-tray printer, same-day scan-backs gets you onto the rotation in one read; one that only says notary public starts an email thread. Add your signing fee and mileage policy so the dispatcher can price the job without ringing you.

Then put the link where they look: in the email signature on every confirmation and scan-back email, as a QR code on the folder that travels with the closing package, and by link to every title office and law firm in your radius. When your expiry rolls over or you add RON, the contact they saved already shows it.

What changes for a notary and has to stay current

A notary commission runs for a fixed term, four years in many states, and comes back with a new expiry date and sometimes a new commission number. E&O policies renew every year. Background screenings for signing work are repeated on the signing services' schedule. You gain RON authorisation, extend into a neighbouring county, raise your travel fee, drop weekend work for a season, or change your number.

Every one of those is printed on something: the cards you left at three title companies, the sticker on your signing folder, the flyer at the care home, the magnet on your car door. Nobody reprints all of that for a renewal. With a digital card each printed QR code opens the same live card, so you edit the expiry once and the card left on a front desk two years ago is right again.

State rules on how a notary may advertise

A notary is a public officer and your card is advertising, so your commissioning authority may have a view on it. The rules differ by state, so check your state's notary handbook or statute before you publish. Points to look for: several states forbid the words notario or notario publico in advertising because of what the title implies in Spanish-speaking communities; some require a statement that you are not an attorney and cannot give legal advice, sometimes in the language of the ad; some restrict reproducing your seal on marketing; and some regulate how notarial and travel fees may be quoted.

The title companies and signing services that book you may add their own requirements on how you describe the service. If a rule changes or your state issues new guidance, correct the wording once and every printed QR code follows.

Setting up a notary's card

  1. Open the builder and enter your name, Notary Public or Notary Public and Loan Signing Agent as the job title, and your business name as the company.
  2. Add your direct mobile number, your email, and your signing-request page as the booking link, with your base town so the service area makes sense.
  3. Put your commission state, number and expiry, your E&O amount and RON status in the tagline, then list the counties you cover, your hours and the languages you sign in.
  4. Add a photo and your business colour, then share by QR code on your signing folder, by link to title companies and signing services, and in your email signature on every confirmation.

Frequently asked questions from notaries

What should a notary's digital business card include?

Your commission state, number and expiry date, whether you do loan signings and hold a current background screening, your E&O amount, whether you offer remote online notarization, your service counties, hours, languages and a direct number or booking link. Check your state's notary advertising rules before you publish.

How does a mobile signing agent get title companies to save the card?

Send the link to the escrow officers and vendor coordinators who book you, print the QR code on the folder that travels with each closing package, and add the link to your email signature on confirmation and scan-back emails. It opens in their phone with your commission and coverage visible.

What happens to the printed QR code when my commission renews?

Nothing needs reprinting. The QR code and link open the live card, so you change the expiry date, commission number or E&O amount once and every card, folder sticker and flyer already out there shows the current details.

Should I show both mobile and remote online notarization on the card?

Only what your state has authorised you for. If you hold a RON authorisation, name it and the platform so lenders who need remote closings can find you; if you are mobile only, say so, and no signing service books you for the wrong appointment type.
