How a personal trainer actually fills a diary

Almost nobody books a personal trainer from an advert. A member watches you coach someone through a deadlift for six weeks and finally asks what you charge. A client tells her sister, who is nervous about walking into a gym alone. A physio finishing a knee rehab course wants the patient to keep lifting under someone who understands the injury. A gym manager needs cover for the 6am small group.

Each of those conversations happens somewhere with no pen and no pockets: the gym floor between sets, the car park after bootcamp, a treatment room, a text forwarded that evening. Your details have to survive that hand-off, because the person who books is often not the member you spoke to.

When the referred friend opens the card, three things decide whether they book: what you specialise in, where and when you train, and how to book a first session without a phone call. A digital card answers all three, and because the QR code, link or email signature open the live version, the open slots they see are this month's.

What goes on a personal trainer's card

  • Where you train. The gym you are based at with a directions link, or that you travel to clients' homes, plus outdoor and online options, so nobody books a session you cannot deliver.
  • Your specialisms, in the client's words. Strength for beginners, fat loss, pre- and post-natal, over-50s, returning after injury. People pick a trainer by need, not by certificate.
  • Which training slots are open. Early mornings full, three lunchtimes open, Saturday small group has space. Put it in the tagline and change it as the diary moves.
  • A booking or consultation link. A free-consultation booking page or a tap-to-text number, so the member books before they reach the changing room.
  • Packages and how you price. Single sessions, blocks of ten, monthly coaching, online plans, with a from-price if you are happy to show one, so the what-do-you-charge conversation happens once.
  • Proof a nervous first-time gym-goer can look at. A link to your results page, client stories or training Instagram, so someone who has never lifted sees people like them.
  • Qualifications, insurance and first aid. Your certification and its issuing body, public liability insurance, and a current first aid certificate. Gyms renting floor space and physios sending patients both ask.

What a physio or a referring client needs to see on a trainer's card

A good share of a trainer's new clients arrive through someone else. Physiotherapists, chiropractors and osteopaths discharge patients who need to keep training under supervision. Existing clients bring partners, colleagues and running-club friends.

A physio sending a patient wants to see that you work alongside a rehab plan, which cases you will and will not take, and a way to reach you that is not your Instagram inbox. Name the experience in plain words, such as post-op knees or older adults, add an email for clinic referrals, and say you follow the physio's programme rather than replace it.

A referred friend needs less: your face, the gym, the specialism they were told about, and the booking link. Email the link to every clinic you want to hear from and put it in your email signature. When you move gyms or add online coaching, the card the physio saved last year already shows it.

What changes in a personal trainer's week and has to stay current

A trainer's details change more often than most. You move from a chain gym to an independent one, or rent floor time at two. Your 6am slots fill and your lunchtimes empty. You add a Saturday bootcamp for the summer and drop it when the clocks change. Prices move in January, a block of ten becomes a monthly plan. Your number changes when you stop using the gym's phone.

Each of those details is printed somewhere: the QR sticker on your water bottle, the flyer on the gym noticeboard, the poster at the physio's reception. Nobody reprints the flyer because Tuesday lunchtime opened up. With a digital card every one of those QR codes points at the same live card: change the availability line once and the sticker on your bottle is right again.

Gym rules, certifying bodies and health claims on a trainer's card

Personal training is not licensed the way dentistry is, but three sets of rules touch your card. First the gym's: many gyms bar freelance trainers from approaching members on the floor unless they rent space or are on the gym's books, so check your agreement before you show a QR code to members. Then your certifying body's: most say how you may describe yourself and whether you may use their logo, so use the exact title they grant. Then advertising rules on health claims: before-and-after photos, weight-loss promises and testimonials are treated differently by country, and some US states restrict individualised nutrition advice to licensed dietitians, so check your state or national rule before you describe nutrition coaching.

If a gym changes its policy, your certificate is renewed under a new title, or a claim has to come off, edit the card once and every QR code follows.

Setting up a personal trainer's card

  1. Open the builder and enter your name with the title your certifying body grants, personal trainer or your specialism as the job title, and your trading name or home gym as the company.
  2. Add the number clients text you on, the gym's address so directions open in maps, your consultation page as the booking link, and an email for physio referrals.
  3. Put your specialisms and open slots in the tagline, link your packages, results and training Instagram, and note your insurance and first aid status.
  4. Add a photo of you coaching and your brand colour, then share by QR code on your water bottle and the gym noticeboard, by link to clinics and clients, and in your email signature.

Frequently asked questions from personal trainers

What should a personal trainer's digital business card include?

Where you train, your specialisms in the client's words, which slots are open, a booking or consultation link, your packages, a link to results or client stories, and your certification, insurance and first aid status. Check your gym's rules on approaching members before you show it on the floor.

How do personal trainers share a digital business card on the gym floor?

Keep the QR code on your phone's lock screen or on a sticker on your water bottle and show it when a member asks what you charge. They scan it, see your specialisms, open slots and booking link, and save your number before they reach the changing room.

What happens when I change gyms or my availability fills up?

Edit the card. The QR code on your water bottle, the noticeboard flyer, the car magnet and the poster at the physio's reception all point at the live card, so the new gym and the open slots appear everywhere the moment you save, with nothing reprinted.

Can I put my certification, before-and-after photos and nutrition coaching on the card?

Use the exact title your certifying body grants and check whether you may show its logo. Before-and-after photos and testimonials fall under advertising rules that differ by country, and some US states restrict individualised nutrition advice to licensed dietitians, so check your state or national rule before describing nutrition coaching. If a claim has to come off, edit the card once and every QR code follows.
