Why recruiters hand out their details differently

A recruiter's contact details travel to two audiences at once. Candidates need to know who you are, which roles or sectors you place, and how to send a CV. Hiring managers and clients need to know what you specialise in and why they should take your call. Most recruiters hand the same details to both, dozens of times a week, on calls, in messages and across a table at a career fair.

Much of that handover happens where paper does badly. A candidate on a video interview cannot take a card from you; a graduate at a busy fair stand takes ten cards and loses nine; a hiring manager forwards your details to a colleague by message. A link or a QR code that opens in any browser and saves you into contacts in one tap works in all three.

Recruiters also move: desks change, agencies rebrand, an independent recruiter changes the sectors they cover or their booking link. A printed card is wrong the day that happens. A live card is edited once, and every QR code and link you have already shared shows the new details.

What a recruiter's card should carry

  • The roles and sectors you place. “Finance and accounting roles in the Midlands” lets a candidate and a client both see in a second whether you are the right recruiter; “talent solutions” does not.
  • A direct number and the hours you take calls. Candidates often call on a lunch break, so say when you are reachable.
  • Where to send a CV. An email address or a link to your application or jobs page, so a candidate does not have to ask.
  • A booking link for a first call. For candidates and hiring managers alike, a scheduling link turns “let's talk” into a time in the diary.
  • Your agency, or your own name if you work independently. With the logo and website, so a client can check who you are before they reply.
  • Your professional profiles. A link to your LinkedIn profile and to current openings, which is where most candidates will look next anyway.

Frequently asked questions

What should a recruiter's digital business card include?

The roles and sectors you place, a direct number and the hours you take calls, where to send a CV, a booking link for a first call, your agency name and logo or your own name if you work independently, and a link to your LinkedIn profile and current openings.

How do recruiters use a digital business card at a career fair?

Show the card's QR code on the stand, a badge or your phone. A candidate points their camera at it, the card opens in the browser and they can save you in one tap and see where to send a CV. On Pro, lead capture lets them send their own name and details back from your card.

Do candidates need an app to open my card?

No. The card opens in any browser from a QR code or a link, and saving you to their contacts is one tap. Only you need an account.

Can a recruitment agency give every recruiter a matching card?

Yes. The Business plan has no seat minimum, so a three-person agency pays for three cards. It adds a central admin dashboard, brand and template lock so every recruiter's card shows the same agency name and logo, and CRM sync.

Is it free for an independent recruiter?

Yes. The free plan includes one digital business card, a QR code and sharing link, your profile, links and socials, an Apple and Google Wallet pass, and unlimited updates — free forever, no credit card. Free cards carry a small CompanyCard credit; removing it is part of Pro. Lead capture, analytics, custom branding and unlimited links and files are part of Pro.
