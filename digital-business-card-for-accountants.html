How an accountant gets clients, and where the card has to travel

An accountant's next client is usually someone an existing client talked to: a café owner tells the tenant next door who does her books, a contractor drops your name in a trade group chat, a client's brother-in-law goes self-employed and needs a tax return before the deadline. In each case the person holding your details is not the person who needs them, so the card has to forward: a link travels in a text or email without being retyped, and the new business owner who opens it sees your name, your qualification and a way to book a first consultation.

The second channel is referral partners: solicitors handling company formations and estates, mortgage brokers whose self-employed borrowers need an accountant's letter, lenders who want two years of accounts. A partner needs a card they can forward while the client is still in the room, and because the link opens the current version, the solicitor never passes on a mobile number you dropped two years ago.

Then there are the rooms where you meet business owners yourself: the chamber of commerce breakfast, the trade association dinner where you are the only accountant at the table. Nobody there wants to type an accountant's email address into a phone while holding a coffee; a QR code on your screen is scanned in a second and opens a page they can send to a business partner that evening.

The card also belongs in the signature on the emails an accountant already sends, from engagement letters to year-end reminders, so a client who wants to recommend you has something to forward.

What a referred client or referring partner needs to see on an accountant's card

  • Your name with the accounting designation you may use. CPA, EA, ACCA, CA or AAT: a referred client was told you are qualified and confirms it here before calling.
  • Firm name and licence or registration number. Some state boards, tax-practitioner registers and professional bodies expect a permit or registration number on marketing material; check your own board's rule.
  • The accounting services, by task. Personal tax returns, year-end accounts, bookkeeping, payroll, VAT or sales tax: a business owner forwarded your card wants to know whether you do the one thing they need.
  • Which clients you take. Sole traders, contractors, owner-managed limited companies, landlords, online sellers: naming them stops enquiries from the wrong kind of client.
  • Whether you are taking new clients right now. A card that says new bookkeeping clients yes, tax returns after the deadline, is honest in February and can be changed in May.
  • A consultation booking link and a secure document link. A new client's first two actions are booking a call and sending last year's return; give them both routes.

What changes in an accounting practice, and why the card has to keep up

An accounting practice looks stable from outside and changes constantly underneath. From January to the April deadline, or through a self-assessment January, you close to new tax clients and reopen a month later; season hours come and go; a junior takes over bookkeeping onboarding and the first-contact number changes; you switch practice software and the client portal link changes with it. Firms merge or rename, and every printed accountant's card in circulation is wrong overnight.

A digital card absorbs that: the QR code on your letterhead, the link in your email signature and the card a solicitor saved last year open the same live page, so every referral partner and client sees your current new-client status, hours and portal link without anything being reprinted.

The one detail to change carefully is how you describe yourself as an accountant. Designations, the word chartered, how an enrolled agent may describe the credential, and whether a firm permit number belongs on marketing material are governed by your state board, tax-practitioner register or professional body and differ by jurisdiction; check your own body's rule before publishing and again when your licence status changes.

Setting up an accountant's card

  1. Open the builder and enter your name with your designation (CPA, EA, ACCA, CA), your firm name, and your licence or registration number if your board expects it on marketing material.
  2. Add your accounting services by task (tax returns, year-end accounts, bookkeeping, payroll, VAT or sales tax), the client types you take and your current new-client status.
  3. Add your consultation booking link, your secure document upload or client portal link, a direct line for referral partners and your filing-season hours, then your firm logo and colour.
  4. Share it by QR code, link or email signature: put the QR on letterhead and engagement letters, send the link once to each solicitor and lender who refers to you, and add it to every client email signature.

Questions accountants and bookkeepers ask about digital business cards

What should an accountant's digital business card include?

Your name with the designation you hold (CPA, EA, ACCA, CA or equivalent), your firm and licence or registration number where your board expects it, your services by task, the clients you take, whether you are accepting new clients this season, a consultation booking link, a secure document link, and your filing-season phone number and hours.

How do accountants use a digital business card for referrals?

Most new accounting clients arrive because an existing client or a referral partner such as a solicitor, mortgage broker or lender passed your details on. Share the card link once with each partner so they can forward it while the client is still in the room; because it opens your current card, a solicitor who saved you two years ago still forwards the right number and new-client status.

Can I show my CPA, EA or chartered designation and my licence number on the card?

You can, and for a referred client or a lender vetting an accountant they are the first thing worth seeing. What you may call yourself, and whether a permit or registration number must appear on marketing material, is set by your state board, tax-practitioner register or professional body and differs by jurisdiction, so check your own body's rule before the card goes live.

Can I change my new-client status during filing season after the QR code is printed?

Yes: the QR code and link point to the card, not to a copy of it, so an edit shows everywhere the code already exists: letterhead, engagement letters, your email signature and the card a referral partner saved last year. Close to new tax clients in February, reopen in May or change your portal link, and every scan opens the current accountant's card; edits are unlimited.
