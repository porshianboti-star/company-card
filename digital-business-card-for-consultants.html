How consultants actually win work, and where the card fits

Very little consulting work starts cold. A former client mentions your name to a peer, someone hears you on a panel, a founder you met at a meetup forwards your details to the co-founder who holds the budget. The person who received your card is rarely the one who signs the statement of work. Your details get passed on, usually in a chat message, to someone who has never met you. The card’s job is to survive that forward: open cleanly on a phone, say in one line who you help and with what, and put the proof and the booking link one tap below.

The person opening it wants three things settled before they reply. What exactly you do, and not “consultant” but “pricing strategy for B2B software companies” or “change management for hospital mergers”. Whether you have done it for an organisation like theirs. And whether you can start this quarter. A paper card answers the first badly and the other two not at all. A digital card carries the positioning line, links to two or three case studies and a note on when you are next available, and shows the current answer whenever it is opened.

Coaches face the same shape of problem with a different buyer. An HR lead shortlisting executive coaches for a leadership programme wants your credential, your niche and a way to book a chemistry call without a week of email. The same card serves both if it leads with the niche and ends in the calendar.

What a consultant’s card needs to show a prospect

  • Your positioning in one line. “Fractional CFO for venture-backed hardware startups” tells a forwarded contact whether to bother; “business consultant” does not.
  • Your trading entity. Many consultants invoice through an LLC or limited company under a different name from the one they introduce themselves by. Put both, so procurement can match the card to the vendor form.
  • Proof, not a biography. Link two or three case studies. A prospect deciding whether to reply reads one, not ten.
  • A booking link for the intro call. Discovery calls are how consulting is sold. Let the prospect hold a slot while the referral is fresh instead of starting a week of “does Tuesday work?”.
  • LinkedIn. For B2B consulting it is where the prospect checks your history and mutual connections before replying.
  • Credentials, stated the way the awarding body allows. A CMC, PMP, SHRM-SCP, ICF credential or CPA belongs on the card, but each body has its own rule on how the mark may be shown, so check theirs. If you advise on tax, investments or insurance, check your regulator’s rules on marketing material before you publish.
  • Availability and where you work. “Taking new engagements from January” or “remote, UK and EU time zones” are the facts a prospect otherwise has to email you to find out.

Where a consultant’s card gets shared, and what changes

You share the card at the front of the room after a conference talk, when six people arrive at once and there is time for a scan but not a conversation. You paste the link into the meeting chat as a discovery call wraps up. It sits in the footer of every proposal and invoice and in your email signature. And the share that brings the most work is one you never see: a past client forwarding it to a peer who has just described the same problem you solved for them.

What sits on a consultant’s card also changes more often than in most professions. You narrow your niche after a year of similar engagements. Your day rate moves. You incorporate and the trading name changes. You take a six-month engagement and close to new work, then it ends and you are available again. Because the QR code on your March slide and the link in the June proposal both open the live card, the person who follows up in October sees what you do now, not what you did when you made the slide.

Questions consultants ask about digital business cards

What should a consultant's digital business card include?

Your name and trading entity, a one-line statement of who you help and with what, links to two or three case studies, a booking link for an intro call, your LinkedIn profile and a note on when you are next available. If you show a credential such as CMC, PMP or an ICF mark, check that body's display rules, and if you advise on financial, tax or insurance matters, check your regulator's rules on marketing material too.

Can a prospect book a discovery call straight from a consultant's card?

Yes. Add your scheduling page as the Book a call link and a prospect who has just been forwarded your details can hold a slot without an email exchange. That matters most in the hour after a referral, while the interest is fresh.

I change my niche and rates often. Does the card keep up?

Yes. The QR code and link open the card itself, not a copy of it, so when you narrow your positioning, change your trading name, raise your rate or close to new engagements for six months, every deck slide, proposal footer and email signature that already carries the card shows the current version. Edits are unlimited on the free plan.

I coach as well as consult. Should I split them on the card?

Usually not. One card with a positioning line that covers both, separate links to your consulting case studies and your coaching page, and one booking link for either kind of intro call is easier for a referrer to forward than two cards they may mix up.
