Digital business card for electricians

Most of an electrician's work arrives by phone: a homeowner with a tripping fuse board, a landlord who needs a safety certificate, a builder who wants the first fix next week. Your card has to get your number and license into their phone before that call, and stay right when the number changes.

How an electrician actually gets the next job

Electrical work comes from three places: the homeowner who had you out once and now tells the neighbours, the landlord or letting agent who needs periodic inspection reports on a dozen flats, and the builder or kitchen fitter who subs out the electrics on every job. They hire the electrician whose number is already in their phone, so the job of your card is to get saved, not admired.

The homeowner wants to know you are licensed, insured and willing to come out tonight if the power is off. The letting agent wants a name, a license or registration number and an email address to forward to the landlord with the certificate. The builder wants to know whether you do first and second fix, three-phase commercial or only domestic, and how far you travel. A digital card puts all of that on one screen, with your phone number at the top where a thumb lands.

An electrician's card also has to survive time. The homeowner who saves you after a fuse board upgrade may not call for four years, and by then your mobile may have changed or you may have stopped doing callouts. Because the QR code and link open the live card rather than a copy, the details saved in year one still reach you in year five.

What a homeowner or landlord needs to see on an electrician's card

  • Two numbers, not one. A tap-to-call line for quotes and bookings, and a separate 24-hour emergency line if you take callouts. If you do not do emergencies, say so.
  • Your license or scheme registration number, as printed. Homeowners are told to check it and letting agents want it for the landlord's file. Put it in your tagline or a custom field exactly as it appears on the license, and check your state or scheme's rule on how it must appear in advertising.
  • Insured, and for what. Public liability cover is the first question a property manager asks before adding an electrician to the approved trades list.
  • The work you take, in the customer's words. Fuse board upgrades, rewires, EV charger installs, landlord safety certificates, commercial fit-outs. A landlord searching for an inspection report needs to see that phrase.
  • Your service area. Name the towns or the radius from your base, so a callout an hour away is ruled out before the phone rings.
  • A route for the trade. Builders and agents send drawings, board photos and tenant details; add WhatsApp or email so they are not squeezing a job spec into a voicemail.
  • Proof, in one tap. A website, a reviews page or your entry on the scheme's public register, so the homeowner can check you before handing over the keys.

Where an electrician's QR code goes: the van, the site board, the certificate

An electrician's details are printed in more places than almost any other trade's: the van, the board outside the job, the quote, the invoice, the test certificate you leave behind and the label inside the consumer unit. Change your number and every one of them is wrong, and the van costs the most to fix. Put the QR code on them instead, and the code keeps opening the current card while the number behind it changes.

The same code on a certificate does a second job. Periodic inspection reports fall due again, and a landlord going through the paperwork before a new tenancy scans the code and finds the electrician who did the last one. On a quote, it lets a homeowner comparing three electricians save yours and check the license number before deciding. In your email signature, the link goes out under every quote and invoice without printing anything.

Treat an electrician's card as advertising. Some state licensing boards require the contractor's license number on every advertisement, and some competent-person schemes have rules on how their mark and your registration may be shown, so check your own board or scheme's rule before you publish. Update the card the day a registration renews or lapses so the number and status stay truthful.

Setting up your electrician's card

  1. Open the builder and enter your name, company and a job title that says what you are, such as licensed electrician or electrical inspector. Put your license or registration number and the word insured in the tagline so they show without scrolling.
  2. Add the number you answer on site as the first field, a second phone or WhatsApp for the 24-hour emergency line and for photos of a fuse board, an email address for landlords' certificate requests, and your base address or service area.
  3. Add your website, a custom link to your reviews page or your entry on the scheme's public register, and a cover image of a finished board or install rather than a stock photo.
  4. Download the QR code for the van, site boards, quotes, invoices and certificates, paste the link into your email signature, and text the link to any customer who asks for your number.

Electricians' questions about digital business cards

What should an electrician's digital business card include?

Your name and company, a tap-to-call number and a separate emergency line if you take callouts, your license or scheme registration number as it appears on the license, whether you are insured, the electrical work you take, your service area, and a link to your website or reviews. Check your state or scheme's rule on what must appear in advertising before you publish.

Can I show my electrical license or registration number on the card?

Yes. Put it in the tagline or a custom field so it sits near your name, typed exactly as it appears on your license so a homeowner or letting agent can look it up on the public register. Check your own board's rule on advertising, and update the card when the license renews.

Where should an electrician put the QR code?

On the van, the board outside a job, your quotes and invoices, and the test certificate you leave behind. A homeowner scans it with the phone camera, the card opens in the browser with nothing to install, and saving your number is one tap.

What happens when I change my number, move areas or add a second van?

You edit the card and every QR code already on the van or on a certificate keeps opening the new details. The van, the certificates in customers' drawers and last year's quotes all point at the same card, so nothing needs reprinting. Edit the service area or number and it is live at the next scan.

Do homeowners and builders need an app to open an electrician's card?

No. They scan the QR code with the normal camera app or tap the link in a text or email, and the card opens as a web page. The save-contact button adds your number, email and company to their phone, which is where an electrician needs to be when the power goes off.
