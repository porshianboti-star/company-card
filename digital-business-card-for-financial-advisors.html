Why an advisor's details are handed on by someone else

An independent financial advisor's next client usually arrives through someone else: a happy client who mentions you to a colleague, an accountant or solicitor who refers a client, a friend who passes your name on at a dinner. Each of those introductions starts with your contact details being handed on by a person who is not you.

Paper does that job badly. The card a client took at their first meeting is in a drawer two years later, and the referral partner who promised to pass your details on has to dig out an old email. A link they can forward in a message, or a QR code that saves you into contacts in one tap, travels with the introduction.

Advisors' details also change: you move to a new network or firm, add a qualification, change your booking link or the areas you focus on. A printed card is wrong the day that happens. A live card is edited once, and every link and QR code you have already shared shows the new details.

What a financial advisor's card should carry

  • The people you help and what you help them with. “Retirement planning for business owners and their families” tells a referred prospect in a second whether you are the right advisor; a generic “wealth solutions” does not.
  • Your firm, or your own name if you are independent. With the logo and website, so a prospect can check who you are before the first call.
  • Your qualifications and the details you are expected to show. Designations, firm or registration details and any wording your firm or compliance reviewer asks for. Have the card approved the same way as the rest of your marketing.
  • A booking link for an introductory meeting. It turns “you should talk to my advisor” into a time in the diary.
  • A direct number and an email address. Clients phone when something changes in their life, so make the direct line easy to find.
  • Your professional profiles and website. A link to your LinkedIn profile and your firm page, which is where a referred prospect looks next anyway.

Frequently asked questions

What should a financial advisor's digital business card include?

Who you help and what you help them with, your firm name and logo or your own name if you are independent, your qualifications and any details your firm or compliance reviewer asks you to show, a booking link for an introductory meeting, a direct number and email, and a link to your LinkedIn profile and website.

Does CompanyCard check my card's wording for compliance?

No. CompanyCard is the card and the sharing; it does not review what you write. Treat the card like the rest of your marketing and have its wording approved by whoever reviews your materials. Because the card is live, a change they ask for is one edit, and every link and QR code you have already shared shows it.

How does a digital business card help with referrals?

A client or referral partner can forward your card link in a message or an email, and the person they introduce opens it in any browser and saves you to their contacts in one tap. On Pro, lead capture lets that person send their own name and details back from your card.

Do my clients need an app to open my card?

No. The card opens in any browser from a QR code or a link, and saving you to their contacts is one tap. Only you need an account.

Is it free for an independent financial advisor?

Yes.
