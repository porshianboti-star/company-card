Digital business card for dentists

Dentistry is local and referred: a patient recommends you, a GP mentions your name, a general dentist sends a case to a specialist. Your card has to survive that hand-off — and still show whether you're taking new patients when it's finally opened.

How a dentist actually gets new patients

Very few people pick a dentist from a cold search. A family moves into the area and asks a neighbour. A hygienist at a school screening mentions the dentist she works for. A general dentist tells a patient with a cracked molar to see a particular endodontist. Each time, your details are passed on by someone who is not the patient, often days before the patient acts.

That is the journey the card has to survive: forwarded in a text message, scanned from a referral slip at another practice's front desk. Whoever opens it needs the practice named unmistakably, the address with directions and a booking route one tap away, with nothing to install.

Before a new patient calls, they settle three things: where you are, whether you are taking new patients, and whether you take their plan. A digital card carries all three, and because the QR code, link or email signature all open the live version, the new-patient line is never out of date.

What goes on a dentist's card

  • The practice, front and centre. Practice name, address and a directions link. Patients choose a dentist they can reach between work and the school run.
  • Whether you are accepting new patients. Put it in the tagline and change it the day the book opens or closes.
  • A booking link or tap-to-call number. Add the practice's online booking page so the card ends in an appointment, not a note to ring later.
  • Insurance and payment options. The plans you are in network with, the public scheme if you take it, payment plans if you offer them, so patients rule themselves in or out before they call.
  • What you do, specifically. General dentistry, hygiene, orthodontics, implants, sedation, children, so a referring dentist knows what to send you.
  • A referral route for other dentists. A direct line or referral email so a general dentist can send a case without joining the patient phone queue.
  • Emergency arrangements. An out-of-hours number or instructions. A patient with a knocked-out tooth on a Saturday opens the card they saved, not your website.

What a referring dentist needs to see on a specialist's card

If you are an orthodontist, periodontist, endodontist, oral surgeon or children's dentist, the patient is not your customer. The referring general dentist and their front desk are, and they send the case to the specialist whose details are already in the phone.

So the card should say what cases you take (molar root canals, wisdom teeth under sedation, implant placement), how to send a referral (a referral email, a direct line that bypasses the patient queue), and how long a new referral waits. Saying you return the patient with a written report answers the question every general dentist has.

Email the link to every practice in your referral radius and put it in your email signature so every case report carries it. When the wait shortens or an associate starts taking cases, the card they saved already shows it.

What changes at a dental practice and has to stay current

The new-patient book opens and closes several times a year. An associate joins, a hygienist starts on Thursdays, a partner retires. You join an insurance network at renewal or leave one. The practice moves, opens a second site, or swaps booking systems and the old booking URL dies.

Every one of those details is printed somewhere: appointment cards, recall postcards, the referral pad you left at the practice down the road, the sign at reception, the sticker on the hygienist's sample bag. Nobody reprints all of that for one closed book. With a digital card the QR code on each of those items points at one live card: change the new-patient line once and the referral slip printed last spring is right again.

Dental board rules on titles, specialties and advertising

Dentistry is a regulated profession and your card is marketing material. The rules differ by country and state, so check each point with your dental board or council. Specialist titles such as orthodontist or periodontist are commonly reserved for dentists registered in that specialty; a general dentist who fits braces may need different wording. Cosmetic dentist is not a listed specialty in many jurisdictions. Some boards restrict the degree letters you show, ask for a registration number on marketing, or object to words like painless.

Before-and-after photos and patient testimonials are treated differently from one board to the next. If the guidance changes, or a colleague points out a title you should not be using, correct the card once and every printed QR code shows the new version.

Setting up a dentist's card

  1. Open the builder and enter your name with the qualification letters your board permits, your role as the job title, and the practice as the company.
  2. Add the practice phone, the address so directions open in maps, the online booking page as your booking link, and a referral email if other dentists send you cases.
  3. Put your new-patient status and the plans you accept in the tagline, link your website's insurance page, and add your emergency arrangements.
  4. Add a profile photo and the practice's theme colour, then share by QR code on referral slips and the reception sign, by link to referring practices, and in your email signature.

Frequently asked questions from dentists

What should a dentist's digital business card include?

The practice name and address, whether you are accepting new patients, a booking link or direct number, the plans you accept, the treatments you provide, an emergency arrangement, and a referral route if other dentists send you cases. If you show a specialist title or qualifications, check your dental board's advertising rules first.

How do dental specialists use a digital business card for referrals?

For an orthodontist, oral surgeon, periodontist or endodontist the audience is referring general dentists and their front desks. Email them the link so it lands in their phones, print the QR code on your referral pad, and add the link to your email signature. It always opens the current card, so the route stays right when your associates or waiting time change.

Can I change my details after the QR code is printed on referral slips and appointment cards?

Yes. The QR code and link point to the card, not to a snapshot of it. Close the book to new patients, change your hours, add an associate or update your insurance list, and the referral slips, appointment cards, recall postcards and reception sign all show the current version.

Can a dentist put specialist titles and qualifications on the card?

Only what your dental board or council allows. Many boards reserve titles such as orthodontist for dentists registered in that specialty, and some restrict degree letters or require a registration number on marketing. Check your board's guidance before you publish; if it changes, edit the card once and every printed QR code follows.
